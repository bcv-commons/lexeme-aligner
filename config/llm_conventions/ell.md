Target positions are the letter runs of the verse in reading order. Accents and breathings are removed from the displayed words
(`αρχη` for `Αρχή`), punctuation is dropped, and an apostrophe of elision splits a word (`δι'` is `δι`, `απ'` is `απ`). Position
numbers refer to that tokenization.

Modern Greek notes (Vamvas 1850, a learned "katharevousa" register close to the New Testament's own Greek; drafted 2026-10-21, not
yet checked against a gold alignment):

- **Much of the New Testament is carried over word for word.** When the translation keeps the source word (`ευαγγελίου` for
  εὐαγγελίου, `Ιησού` for Ἰησοῦ), that word is its rendering, even when the form or the spelling differs a little. Do not let the
  similarity pull a source word onto the wrong occurrence: match by position in the clause, not only by spelling.
- **The article is a separate word** (`ο`, `η`, `το`, `του`, `της`, `τον`, `την`, `οι`, `τα`, `των`, `τους`, `τας`) and goes to the
  Greek article it renders; an article the translator added before a name or a noun with no Greek article belongs to that noun.
- **Prepositions** (`εν`, `εις`, `εκ` / `εξ`, `από`, `δια`, `μετά`, `περί`, `προς`, `υπό`, `επί`, `παρά`, `κατά`) go to the Greek
  preposition they render; a Greek dative or genitive with no preposition, rendered with one (`εις τον`, `από`, `δια`), gives that
  preposition to the noun.
- **Conjunctions and particles**: `και` = καί, `δε` = δέ, `αλλά` = ἀλλά, `διότι` / `επειδή` / `ότι` = ὅτι / γάρ as the sense
  requires, `ίνα` / `διά να` / `να` = ἵνα (a `να` before a verb for a Greek infinitive or subjunctive belongs to that verb), `θέλει`
  + verb = a Greek future (both words go to the verb).
- **Pronouns**: the short forms (`μου`, `σου`, `του`, `της`, `μας`, `σας`, `των`, `τον`, `την`, `με`, `σε`) are separate words and go
  to the Greek pronoun they render. A Greek subject pronoun with no word of its own is carried by the verb ending: that is "carried
  inside" the verb, not "left out".
- **Verb forms**: a periphrastic form (`είχε γράψει`, `θέλει έλθει`, `ήτο διδάσκων`) belongs entirely to the one Greek verb.
- **Old Testament** (source is Hebrew): the Hebrew prefixes ו ה ב ל מ כ ש are separate source words in our Hebrew text; each goes to
  the Greek word that renders it (`και`, the article, `εν` / `εις` / `από` / `ως`), or is "left out" when the translation has none.
  `את` (object marker) has no Greek word; the divine name יהוה is `Κύριος`.
