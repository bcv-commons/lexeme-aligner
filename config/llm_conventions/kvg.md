Target positions are the letter runs of the verse in reading order (Latin script). Punctuation is dropped, and a hyphen splits a
word: `Keliso-gi` is `Keliso` + `gi`, `Deivid-gé` is `Deivid` + `gé`. Position numbers refer to that tokenization.

Kuni-Boazi notes (Mbumbukiam-qa Manqat Mbomambaq, Wycliffe 2015; a Papuan language of Western Province, Papua New Guinea; drafted
2026-10-23 from the statistical alignment only, NOT checked by a speaker or against a gold alignment — treat them as hints):

- **Hyphenated endings are their own positions.** A short form after a hyphen (`gi`, `gé`, `za`, `qa` ...) usually marks the
  relation of the word before it (possession, location, topic). When a Greek or Hebrew function word (a genitive article, a
  preposition, a possessive pronoun) has no separate word, the ending that carries its relation goes to that source word;
  otherwise the ending goes with the word it is attached to.
- **Frequent linking words** in the existing alignment: `neka` / `Neka` and `Yaq` / `yaq` often render καί / δέ; `Geté` often
  renders δέ; `te` / `té` often renders a preposition (ἐν, εἰς, ἐκ, ἐπί, πρός); `mokho` appears with ἐν. Check each case in its
  clause; these are tendencies, not rules.
- **Pronouns**: `no`, `ni`, `noqa`, `nigi` appear for ἐγώ; `zo`, `qo`, `zoqa` for σύ. A Greek pronoun with no word of its own may be
  carried by the verb; mark it carried inside the verb only when the verb form clearly carries it, else leave it out.
- **Discourse words** (`ndøgo`, `ndǿgo`, `ndego`, `ndigu`, `ezoqam`, `Zapa ndǿgo`) are frequent and often render a Greek particle or
  article (γάρ, ὅτι, ὁ) or nothing at all. Do not give them to a content word they do not render.
- **Names** keep a recognisable spelling (`Yesu`, `Keliso`, `Deivid`, `Eibleem`): match them by position as well as by spelling.
- **Old Testament** (Ruth, Jonah; source is Hebrew): the Hebrew prefixes ו ה ב ל מ כ ש are separate source words in our Hebrew text;
  each goes to the word that renders it or is left out. `את` (object marker) has no rendering of its own.
